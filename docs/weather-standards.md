# Weather Standards

## Purpose

Weather should support the mission's visual and tactical intent without unnecessarily degrading player situational awareness or carrier operations.

## Default template philosophy

- Use a consistent base-weather preset for ordinary missions.
- Prefer morning or late-afternoon lighting that provides depth and atmosphere without placing harsh sunlight in the pilot's primary view.
- Check lighting from the actual player start position and expected ingress, target and recovery directions.
- Treat wind direction carefully: confirm how the current DCS Mission Editor displays and applies each wind layer rather than relying on memory.

## Time and lighting

Before finalising mission time:

1. View the cockpit from each principal player slot.
2. Check the sun during launch and initial climb.
3. Check the expected target direction.
4. Check the carrier approach and groove.
5. Confirm that briefing images and map visibility remain useful.

Avoid a low sun near the normal one-o'clock scan position unless it is a deliberate mission feature.

## Wind

- Configure surface and upper-level winds deliberately.
- Record whether the Mission Editor value represents wind direction in the way expected for the installed DCS version.
- Consider ambient wind together with carrier heading and speed.
- Avoid abrupt, unrealistic changes between layers unless required by the scenario.
- Recheck tanker groundspeed and track after changing wind.

## Carrier weather

- The carrier must have suitable wind over deck for the intended launch and recovery experience.
- Carrier speed and heading should not be chosen independently of the weather.
- Confirm that waves, deck movement and visibility remain appropriate for the group's desired difficulty.
- Test the full approach in the selected aircraft rather than judging conditions only from the Mission Editor.

## Clouds and visibility

- Cloud bases should not unintentionally block the target, tanker, marshal stack or approach.
- Cloud cover should add tactical interest without making AI behaviour unusable.
- Visibility and haze should suit the map and mission scale.
- Fog should be used sparingly and tested in multiplayer.

## Turbulence and precipitation

- Use turbulence only when it adds meaningful atmosphere or challenge.
- Avoid settings that make formation, refuelling or carrier recovery frustrating without narrative benefit.
- Rain and thunderstorms require a complete performance and visibility test.
