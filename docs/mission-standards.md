# Mission Standards

## Purpose

These standards define the default design approach for Super Sonic DCS missions. They are a starting point rather than an inflexible rulebook. Individual missions may depart from them where the scenario benefits.

## Audience and duration

- Primary audience: two or three human players flying together.
- Default aircraft: carrier-based F-14 variants.
- Normal session length: two to three hours including briefing, startup and recovery.
- Missions should remain playable when one expected player is absent where practical.

## Design philosophy

1. **Fun and reliability first.** Realistic procedures should improve immersion rather than create avoidable frustration.
2. **Clear player purpose.** The briefing must explain what the players are doing, why it matters and what constitutes success.
3. **Credible opposition.** Enemy forces should feel plausible without depending on perfect AI behaviour.
4. **Recoverable missions.** A single missed trigger, damaged support asset or late player should not unnecessarily make the scenario impossible.
5. **Controlled complexity.** Add scripts and advanced logic only when they provide a clear gameplay benefit.
6. **Carrier operations are part of the mission.** Launch and recovery should be designed and tested, not treated as an afterthought.

## Standard mission phases

A typical mission should provide:

1. Briefing and aircraft preparation.
2. Carrier launch or airfield departure.
3. Rendezvous and package formation.
4. Ingress with navigation, threat and fuel considerations.
5. Primary task such as strike, CAP, interception or support.
6. Egress and optional secondary events.
7. Recovery with tanker support where appropriate.
8. Clear mission completion or debrief indication.

## Player experience standards

- Avoid unnecessary waiting before the first meaningful decision or action.
- Use hot starts by default for normal Wednesday-night missions unless startup is part of the experience.
- Give players enough information to plan, but avoid revealing every scripted event.
- Provide sensible alternatives when a support asset is unavailable.
- Avoid forcing players to use external documents for essential mission information; include critical data in the briefing or kneeboard.

## Difficulty

- Difficulty should come from tactical decisions, coordination and execution rather than hidden information or brittle triggers.
- Enemy skill levels should be selected deliberately and tested.
- Large numbers of AI aircraft should not substitute for thoughtful threat design.
- Mission success should not require every secondary objective unless explicitly briefed.

## Technical standards

- Use unique, descriptive group and unit names.
- Keep trigger logic readable and grouped by mission phase.
- Document external scripts and their load order.
- Avoid changing a base template directly; create a development copy.
- Test in multiplayer, not only through the Mission Editor preview.
- Record known DCS or module limitations in the mission notes.

## Completion criteria

A mission is ready for release when:

- The build checklist has been completed.
- The briefing and kneeboard information agree with the mission settings.
- Player slots, radios, navigation aids and support aircraft have been verified.
- Primary objectives and completion logic have been tested.
- Launch and recovery have been flown successfully in multiplayer.
- Known limitations are documented.
